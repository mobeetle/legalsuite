# frame — capability

**Contract.** Restate the request, decompose it, separate the load-bearing gaps,
ask them once, and name what would collapse the option set. It runs first, on
every path, and carries no tools — it is pure judgment.

`problem_solver` = `frame` + `orchestrate`. It is framed once and orchestrated
throughout; the two stay separate because their lifecycles differ.

## Method

1. **Restate.** One or two sentences: what the user is actually trying to
   accomplish (the Gate Plan's `RESTATED REQUEST`). If the restatement is wrong,
   nothing downstream is right. The restatement always yields the **session
   goal**: one primary outcome (the `GOALS` G1) plus its **done-definition**
   (what `done` means — the `SUCCESS CRITERIA` that close it). No plan or work
   starts with an `idea reframed` alone; the goal and its done-definition are
   part of the frame.
2. **Decompose.** Break the request into the parts that can be worked and checked
   separately, and name the object of work (a document, an assessment, a
   question, a case, an organisation, a process).
3. **Separate the gaps.** A gap is **load-bearing** when it changes the numbers,
   the parties, the strategy, the deliverable or the outcome. Load-bearing gaps
   are questions, never inferences: ask them once, in one card, and stop
   (`gate.md`, *Clarification gate*). Non-load-bearing gaps become `[ASSUMED]`
   with a basis and are surfaced, not asked.
4. **Collapse the option set.** Where the matter is open-ended, name the one or
   two facts that would decide between the options, and ask those rather than
   presenting a menu.

## Checks

- A restatement exists before any plan or work.
- The restatement names the session goal (primary outcome) and its done-definition.
- Every load-bearing gap is asked, not inferred; every non-load-bearing gap is
  tagged `[ASSUMED]` with its basis.
- The option-collapsing question is named wherever the matter is open-ended.
